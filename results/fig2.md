## What the X1/X2/X3 datasets are

Three 5k test sets generated by
`simulate_and_extract/simulate_and_extract_diverse_population_X{1,2,3}.sh`.
Two parameters change relative to the 100k training simulation:

| dataset | POP_MIN | POP_MAX | SAMPLE_RATE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100k train | 5,000 | 50,000 | 0.0002–0.0028 (swept) |
| X1 | 5,000 | 50,000 | 0.0015 (fixed) |
| X2 | 10,000 | 100,000 (×2) | 0.00075 (fixed) |
| X3 | 15,000 | 150,000 (×3) | 0.0005 (fixed) |

Crucially, `POP × SAMPLE_RATE` is held essentially constant (~7.5 expected
samples per outbreak) across all three. The **expected tree size is
preserved** — only the **host-population scale** changes. X1/X2/X3 is a
clean OOD probe along a single axis.

## The headline finding

Across X1 → X2 → X3:

- **Accuracy drops** (Row 3, y-axis) — expected for OOD shift.
- **CP interval widths stay essentially flat** (Row 3, x-axis):

  | target | pipeline | X1 | X2 | X3 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | reg_r0 (width) | stephy   | 1.27 | 1.31 | 1.32 |
  | reg_r0 (width) | CBLV-CNN | 2.16 | 2.23 | 2.25 |
  | reg_rr (width) | stephy   | 0.026 | 0.025 | 0.024 |
  | reg_sss (width)| stephy   | 0.34 | 0.34 | 0.33 |
  | cls_as (set size)| stephy | 3.15 | 3.32 | 3.43 |

- **Empirical coverage collapses** (Row 4):

  | target | pipeline | X1 cov | X2 cov | X3 cov |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | reg_r0 | CBLV-CNN | 0.96 | 0.94 | 0.92 |
  | reg_r0 | stephy   | 0.96 | 0.85 | **0.77** |
  | reg_rr | CBLV-CNN | 0.95 | 0.87 | **0.78** |
  | reg_rr | stephy   | 0.95 | **0.60** | **0.38** |
  | reg_sss| both     | ~0.95 | ~0.94 | ~0.92 |
  | cls_as | both     | ~0.99 | ~0.99 | ~0.99 |

So uncertainty width — the field a user would naturally trust as a
confidence signal — gives **no warning** that the model has moved
off-distribution. Only empirical coverage (which requires labels on the
test set, i.e. a luxury you do not have at deployment time) reveals the
failure.

## Why this happens — the mechanism

CP calibration produces a single offset (`qhat` for CQR; threshold τ̂ for
RAPS) on the calibration split, applied identically to all three test sets.
Per-prediction interval width is then:

- **CQR**: `width(x) = (q_hi(x) − q_lo(x)) + 2·qhat`
- **RAPS**: set size depends on the softmax tail relative to a fixed τ̂

Two reasons the model-emitted spread `q_hi − q_lo` doesn't grow on X2/X3:

1. **Tree topology is held constant by design.** Because `POP × SAMPLE_RATE`
   is preserved, the tip count, CBLV vector length, and tree depth in
   rate-time units look essentially the same to the model. Its quantile
   heads have no input signal saying "this is OOD" — they emit the spread
   they were trained to emit.
2. **The features the model consumes are scale-invariant in the relevant
   axes.** CBLV + tree-stat aux features are normalized/topological;
   absolute host-population scale doesn't enter directly. The model's
   *point prediction* drifts because the underlying R0/RR mapping shifts
   under different pop scales, but the *per-input quantile spread* doesn't.

So you get accurate predictions paired with stable widths in-distribution,
and biased predictions paired with the same stable widths out-of-distribution.

## Is this a CQR weakness?

No — it is a property of **all split conformal methods** (CQR, RAPS,
vanilla split CP, jackknife+, CV+). The marginal coverage guarantee
`P(y ∈ interval) ≥ 1 − α` is conditional on **exchangeability** between
calibration and test data. Once the test distribution differs from the
calibration distribution, no theorem applies, and degradation can be
arbitrary.

CQR specifically *looks* worse here because its selling point is conditional
adaptivity: the quantile heads narrow on easy inputs. That sharpness is a
strength in-distribution and a liability under shift — narrow intervals
plus a small `qhat` leave little margin to absorb bias. Vanilla split CP
would have produced wider, less informative, but probably better-covering
intervals on X3 — at the cost of being overly conservative everywhere on
X1.

## The accuracy ↔ robustness Pareto

The pattern in Row 4 is striking: **stephy degrades faster than CBLV-CNN**
on every regression task. This is the same mechanism at the model level:

- stephy is more accurate on X1 → its quantile heads emit sharper spreads
  → calibration produces a smaller `qhat` → less margin to absorb shift
  → coverage breaks first.
- CBLV-CNN is less accurate on X1 → wider calibrated intervals → more
  buffer → coverage degrades more gracefully.

Sharper conditional models give tighter intervals when the assumption
holds and steeper coverage cliffs when it doesn't.

`reg_rr` is the most extreme case: stephy goes from 95% coverage on X1
to 38% on X3. The model is confidently wrong about recovery rate at
larger population scales because recovery-rate inference is most sensitive
to the absolute timescale of the epidemic, which the host-population scale
shifts.

## Why classification (cls_as) is robust

`cls_as` (ancestral state under RAPS) holds at ~99% coverage across all
three scales:

- The ancestral-state task is naturally scale-invariant — which root state
  was ancestral does not depend on the absolute host population.
- RAPS calibration was already over-covering on X1 (likely nominal 90%,
  achieved 99%), giving substantial slack to absorb mild shift.

This is a useful contrast: not every task fails under this shift, only the
ones whose underlying mapping depends on the shifted axis.

## Takeaways for the paper

1. **Width is a deceptive uncertainty signal under distribution shift.**
   In-distribution it tracks model confidence faithfully; out-of-distribution
   it can be both narrow and wrong.
2. **The accuracy/robustness frontier is real here.** The most accurate
   model is also the most overconfident one when the test distribution
   shifts. This has direct implications for model selection if deployment
   conditions are uncertain.
3. **At deployment time, empirical coverage is invisible.** Without ground
   truth, a practitioner sees only the (flat, narrow, reassuring) interval
   width — and would have no signal that anything is wrong.
4. **The fix is not "use a different CP variant".** Distribution-aware
   methods (weighted conformal, online/adaptive CP, distribution-robust
   bounds) require either a known likelihood ratio between train and test,
   or streaming labels for recalibration — neither is realistic for
   one-shot phylogenetic inference on a novel outbreak.

The substantive finding is therefore not about CQR specifically but about
the deployment-time silence of split-CP uncertainty under epidemiologically
plausible distribution shift.
